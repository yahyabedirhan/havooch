Havooch

Pronounced /hah-VOOCH/

Pause the video. Point at the frame.
Your coding agent answers right there.

A native Mac video player for reviewing videos with your coding agent. Comment on any moment, or draw a box around what you mean, press ⌘↩, and your agent gets the time, the frame, the region and what was being said. Then it answers right beside the frame.

Pause on any moment, or drag a region on the frame.

What the agent receives

Each send is grouped by thread. For every thread the agent gets the exact time, the keyframe image, the region you drew and its crop, the transcript from 15 seconds either side, and the context: what the video is about, its source repos and your note. Then it works in your repo, commits, and tells you on the thread what it changed.

The agent side is one skill, havooch-mate. Ask your agent to listen for Havooch feedback, and you'll see it listening in the player.

Features

Built like a Mac app, because it is one.

I built it from native AppKit and SwiftUI parts, with one calm surface across the window and fine hairlines where other apps paint bands.

Every thread, by who acts next

The thread list sorts your review into Needs you, With agent, Queued and Done, so you always know whose turn it is. Each row shows its frame, its state and who spoke last, and the pins on the timeline take you to the same threads.

A real conversation

Your messages sit on the right, your agent's on the left with its logo. Each one shows where it is: queued, sent, acknowledged, working, done or failed.

One composer

One field at the foot of the sidebar does it all: follow up on a thread, answer a question, or start a new note at the playhead. ↩ queues, ⌘↩ sends the queue.

Works with your agent

Any agent that runs skills and a shell can listen, and the player shows you who it is, with its logo.

The agent asks. You answer at once.

When a note could mean two things, your agent asks on the thread instead of guessing. The question gets its own card and moves the thread to Needs you, and your answer skips the queue and goes straight back.

Native surfaces, or your editor's colours

Default Light and Default Dark use the native macOS surfaces. Rather have your editor's colours? Tokyo Night, Dracula, One Dark Pro, Catppuccin, GitHub and more come built in, and a theme file of your own can build on any of them.

  • Agents drive it too. A leased CLI lets an agent open videos, seek, comment and take screenshots, and you can take control back at any time.
  • Reviews stay. Threads are kept per video by its content, so a renamed or moved copy opens the same review.
  • Context travels. A sidecar file or a note in the app tells your agent what the video is about.

Install

One command.

Both ways install the same release build and the havooch command.

Homebrew

Recommended
brew install --cask yahyabedirhan/tap/havooch

Updates with brew upgrade.

Install script

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yahyabedirhan/havooch/main/scripts/install.sh | bash

Checks the release's SHA-256, installs the app, the command and the havooch-mate skill. Remove it all with --uninstall.

First launch. Havooch is signed ad hoc, not notarized, so macOS asks once. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway. Then try the demo from the empty window to see the whole loop, even before you have a video of your own.

The name

Named after a cat.

Havooch is named after Havuç, my orange-and-white cat. Havuç is Turkish for “carrot”, and one look at the cat tells you why.

I spelled the app's name the way it sounds — hah-VOOCH — so you can type it and say it without guessing. The logo is Havuç's head, drawn simply enough to read at 16 pixels.
